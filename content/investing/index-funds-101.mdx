Legendary investor Warren Buffett famously recommended that the average investor should simply buy a low-cost S&P 500 index fund and hold it for decades. Over long horizons, broad market index funds consistently outperform actively managed funds after accounting for management fees.

## Active vs. Passive Funds: Expense Ratio Comparison

| Metric | Active Mutual Funds | Low-Cost Index Funds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Expense Ratio** | 0.75% - 1.50% per year | 0.03% - 0.10% per year |
| **Strategy** | Fund manager stock picks | Replicates target benchmark |
| **10-Year Outperformance** | ~15% of active funds | Tracks market return (~9-10% historical) |
| **Turnover & Taxes** | Higher capital gains distributions | Highly tax efficient |

<Callout type="tip" title="The Power of 1% Saved in Fees">
On a $100,000 portfolio held over 30 years, paying a 1% management fee versus a 0.05% expense ratio can cost you over **$140,000** in lost compound growth.
</Callout>

## How to Get Started with Index Investing

1. **Open a Brokerage Account**: Select a low-cost broker (Vanguard, Fidelity, Charles Schwab, or interactive platforms).
2. **Automate Monthly Contributions**: Set up automatic transfers on payday to practice Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA).
3. **Reinvest Dividends Automatically**: Enable Automatic Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP) to compound growth.
